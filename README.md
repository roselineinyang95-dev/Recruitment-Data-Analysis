## Recruitment-Data-Analysis

## Project Overview
This project analyzes real-time recruitment data from a consulting firm to identify trends in candidate sourcing, selection, and job placement.

## Objectives
- Organize previously paper-based records into a structured database 
- Analyze candidate flow from application to placement
- Identify drop-off points in the recruitment process
- Evaluate effectiveness of sourcing channels
- Provide data-driven recommendations to improve hiring success.

## Dataset: Castel Recruitment Data
The dataset is called Recruitment data. It contains real-time information on candidate data (Applications Received), subscriber data (Candidates in the job pool), and compliance (Candidates successfully placed in jobs) of a Consulting Firm.
The data was entered, cleaned, and prepared by checking for missing values, duplicates, and data inconsistencies. 

## Tools Used
- Excel
- Power BI

## Key Insights
1. A large number of candidates, about 288 (45.93%), came through the “Unknown” medium of reach, making it the highest application channel. Outside of Unknown sources, Online (143; 22.81%) and Referrals (125; 19.94%) are the strongest channels.
2.	The number of candidates that came for interviews skyrocketed in July (163) and August (151); there was a sharp decline in September (86), with volume remaining relatively flat for the remainder of the year.
3.	Front Desk is the most applied-for position, and then Accountant, Admin, and Business Development Manager follow closely behind. Waitress and Customer Service are at the bottom of the Top 10 list.
4.	The gender distribution of applicants appears relatively balanced, with 51.95% female applicants and 48.05% male applicants.

## Recommendation
1.	Implement a mandatory field for ‘medium of reach’ during candidate registration to track the channel. Identifying these sources will help the firm know which job boards or social platforms performed the best. Since Referrals are amongst the Top 3 channels, a referral bonus program should be launched to boost the number further.
2.	The data shows a sharp drop from 163 applications in July to just 86 in September. Investigate whether this was due to reduced job postings or a seasonal industry trend. Increase candidate sourcing campaigns before peak months and also engage clients earlier in the year.
3.	Create a pool of qualified candidates for the high-demand roles so that when clients’ requests for it, candidates are already available.
